The orchestrator chooses which agent responds by interpreting the resident’s message and matching it to the agent whose responsibilities best fit the request. If the message concerns a scheduled obligation, such as a reminder, appointment, or household task, the orchestrator routes it to the agent that maintains the relevant schedule and obligations. If the message depends on remembered facts, preferences, or household context, it may route to the agent responsible for long-term facts or short-term memory. The decision is therefore based on task type, available context, and the agent most likely to provide an accurate and timely response.

The orchestrator also gathers relevant context before generating a reply. This may include short-term memory from recent interactions, long-term facts about the household, and the current schedule of obligations. By assembling this context, the system can respond with information that is specific to the resident and consistent with prior commitments. The goal is not simply to produce a fluent answer, but to produce one that reflects the correct state of the household and the resident’s needs.

Reliability is emphasized over cleverness because household assistance depends on dependable behavior. A missed reminder, incorrect appointment, or forgotten obligation can have real consequences, while a plain but accurate response is usually acceptable. Cleverness may make replies more engaging, but it can also introduce uncertainty, ambiguity, or unnecessary complexity. In this setting, the safest design is one that prioritizes correct routing, consistent memory use, and dependable scheduling.
