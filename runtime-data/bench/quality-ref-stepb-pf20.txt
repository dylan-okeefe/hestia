The orchestrator chooses which agent responds by first interpreting the resident’s message and identifying the task type, such as a reminder, schedule update, household coordination, or general question. It then examines the relevant context, including short-term memory, long-term facts, and the current schedule of obligations, to determine which agent has the appropriate knowledge and responsibility. If multiple agents could respond, the orchestrator selects the one with the strongest match to the request and the most reliable access to the needed information.

The selection process also considers confidence and risk. If the orchestrator is uncertain about the correct agent or the correct action, it may choose a conservative fallback, ask for clarification, or route the request to a general-purpose agent rather than guessing. This prevents the system from taking an incorrect action or giving a misleading answer, especially when the request involves time-sensitive or household-critical tasks.

Reliability is emphasized over cleverness because household assistants often manage obligations that affect people’s daily lives, such as reminders, appointments, chores, and coordination between residents. A missed reminder, wrong schedule update, or incorrect task assignment can cause real inconvenience or harm, even if the assistant’s response sounds impressive. A plain, accurate, and dependable response is therefore more valuable than a creative or speculative one.

In short, the orchestrator prioritizes correct routing, relevant context, and safe behavior. The goal is not to produce the most sophisticated answer, but to consistently understand the resident’s needs and act on them in a way that can be trusted.
