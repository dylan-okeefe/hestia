The orchestrator chooses which agent responds by first interpreting the resident’s message and identifying the task type, such as a reminder, household coordination request, factual question, or scheduling need. It then examines the relevant context: short-term memory for recent conversation, long-term facts for stable household information, and the schedule of obligations for time-sensitive commitments. Based on that context, it selects the agent whose responsibilities and memory best match the request, while also considering confidence, urgency, and whether another agent should be consulted before answering.

If the request is ambiguous or spans multiple domains, the orchestrator may route it to a general coordinator or gather input from several agents before producing a single reply. For example, a message like “Can you remind me to take out the trash before guests arrive?” may involve both scheduling and household-task agents. The orchestrator’s goal is to make the routing decision transparent and conservative, preferring the agent most likely to have the correct obligation or fact rather than guessing creatively.

Reliability is emphasized over cleverness because household assistance involves commitments that can have real consequences if missed. A missed reminder, wrong appointment time, or incorrect household instruction can cause inconvenience, stress, or safety issues. A plain, accurate response that correctly triggers the right obligation is more valuable than a witty or speculative answer that might sound helpful but fails to act reliably.
