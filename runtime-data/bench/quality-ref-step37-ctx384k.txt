The orchestrator chooses which agent responds by interpreting the resident’s message and matching it to the agent whose responsibilities and context are most relevant. It considers the immediate conversation in short-term memory, durable household facts in long-term memory, and any scheduled obligations that may affect the answer. For example, a request about a reminder, appointment, or household task would be routed to the agent that maintains the schedule and can act on it, while a question about a stored preference or fact would be routed to the agent responsible for long-term facts.

The selection process is not just about picking the most “interesting” agent, but about choosing the one most likely to produce a correct, timely, and actionable response. The orchestrator gathers the relevant context before streaming the reply so that the chosen agent can answer with the right details, such as the resident’s name, preferences, prior commitments, or household constraints. This helps prevent mismatches, such as a general assistant answering a scheduling question without checking the calendar.

Reliability is emphasized over cleverness because household assistance depends on trust and consistency. A missed reminder, wrong appointment, or forgotten obligation can cause real inconvenience, even if the assistant’s reply sounds creative or impressive. In this setting, the best response is often plain, accurate, and dependable: it confirms the task, preserves the correct timing, and avoids unnecessary speculation. Cleverness is useful only when it does not compromise correctness, so the system prioritizes predictable behavior and careful context use.
