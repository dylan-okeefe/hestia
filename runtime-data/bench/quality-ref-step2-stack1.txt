The orchestrator chooses which agent responds by interpreting the resident’s message and matching it to the agent whose responsibilities and context are most relevant. It considers the agent’s short-term memory, long-term facts, and schedule of obligations to determine whether the request involves reminders, household coordination, personal preferences, or another task. The goal is to route the message to the agent that can act on the correct information rather than simply the one that sounds most fluent.

Reliability is emphasized over cleverness because household assistance depends on dependable execution. A missed reminder, incorrect schedule update, or forgotten obligation can have real consequences, while a plain but accurate response is usually acceptable. In this setting, consistency and predictability are more valuable than creative or elaborate replies.

This design also reduces the risk of errors caused by ambiguous routing or overconfident generation. By prioritizing clear task handling and verified context, the system favors agents that can reliably complete obligations. The result is a more trustworthy assistant, even if its responses are less sophisticated.
