The orchestrator chooses which agent responds by interpreting the resident’s message and matching it to the agent whose responsibilities and context are most relevant. It considers the intent of the request, the household task involved, and the information each agent maintains, such as short-term memory, long-term facts, and scheduled obligations. If the message concerns a reminder, appointment, or household duty, the orchestrator routes it to the agent that owns that obligation and can access the appropriate schedule or memory.

The selection process is not based on which agent sounds most impressive, but on which agent can provide the most dependable answer. The orchestrator gathers the relevant context before responding so the chosen agent can act from accurate, task-specific information rather than guessing. This reduces the risk of conflicting replies, missed tasks, or responses that sound helpful but are not grounded in the household’s actual state.

Reliability is emphasized over cleverness because household assistance depends on trust and consistency. A resident may rely on reminders, schedules, and coordinated tasks, so a missed or incorrect response can cause real inconvenience. A plain, accurate reply that correctly reflects the obligation is more valuable than a witty or speculative one that might confuse the user or fail to complete the task.

In this system, the goal is to keep the household running smoothly. The orchestrator therefore favors clear routing, relevant context, and dependable execution over elaborate or creative behavior. This design makes the assistant easier to trust, because residents can expect that important tasks will be handled consistently rather than only when the response seems clever.
